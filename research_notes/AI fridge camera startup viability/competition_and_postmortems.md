## 1. Post-mortems: who tried camera- or sensor-based fridge inventory, what they raised, and what stopped them

### Takeaway
None of the consumer attempts found here built a lasting business on fridge inventory. The retrofit cameras either never got recognition working (Smarter FridgeCam, Fridge Eye) or were killed by their parent along with the app (Liebherr HNGRYnsite, which worked with any brand). The "freshness" gadget was really a timer (Ovie), and Amazon quietly dropped its weight-shelf reorder device. The food-waste apps were liquidated (Save Eat, 2025) or sold off cheaply (Kitche to Remy, 2025). The money was small. At the low end were a US$25k crowdfunding goal (Fridge Eye) and about US$64k raised (Ovie). The best-funded player, Smarter, raised roughly US$10–11M of venture funding.

- Five failure modes recur:
  1. Recognition was not good enough before general-purpose vision models arrived.
  2. One fixed camera sees only the front of the shelves.
  3. The core value, "see my fridge from the store", is replicated by a phone photo.
  4. Price and subscription resistance at US$69–149 per camera.
  5. Platform risk: the cloud or parent company goes away and the hardware is bricked.
- The technical reason FridgeCam failed (bad recognition) has largely gone away since 2025, because Gemini-class models recognise 2,000+ foods (section 2). The commercial reasons (low willingness to pay, phone substitutes, cloud dependence) have not.
- An appliance maker (Liebherr) did build a camera for any brand of fridge, and dropped it within roughly a year if the 2024 press-release date is right. That suggests the retrofit economics did not work even with a brand behind them, though Liebherr's reasons were not found.
- Well-funded smart-kitchen companies failed on price and "a manual alternative exists" (Juicero, Teforia). A retrofit fridge camera faces the same test against the free alternative of opening the fridge or taking a photo.

## 2. Incumbents 2025–2027: appliance makers and big tech

### Takeaway
By 2026 every major appliance brand except Whirlpool and Electrolux (as far as found) ships or has announced an in-fridge camera. The leaders have moved to cloud large-language-model vision: Samsung (Gemini, US from May 2026), LG (ThinQ Food on 2026 SIGNATURE models), Hisense (IFA 2026 "AI Companion" fridge), GE Profile (crisper camera plus barcode scanner plus Instacart, US$4,899) and Miele (up to four view-only cameras). All of it ships only inside new, premium fridges. No appliance maker was found selling a retrofit camera now; Liebherr's was killed in 2025. Meanwhile Amazon (Alexa for Shopping) and Google (Gemini Live) are making "photograph your fridge, get a list or cart" a free phone feature.

## 4. Patents: in-fridge cameras, door-triggered tracking, imaging-based spoilage detection, and freedom to operate

### Takeaway
The basic building blocks of a fridge camera have been patented repeatedly by appliance makers since about 2014. They include a camera on the door or ceiling, a shot when the door closes (with a delay so the lens can defog), heated camera modules, infrared imaging, a camera device that sits in a door pocket, upload of images to a server and app, and comparing before and after images to detect added food. Amazon holds a patent family on image-based spoilage sensing. Filings from the early 2000s and a 2008 Samsung grant have probably expired, but many from 2014–2019 probably run into the mid-to-late 2030s. Nothing found is assigned to Smarter or Liebherr, and no filing was found that explicitly claims freshness from how long an item has been in the fridge. **This is not legal advice. A patent lawyer is needed** before selling hardware, especially in the US.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Indicative expiry dates (inference; verify each one).** These use the general US rule of 20 years from the earliest non-provisional filing date, before any term adjustment or disclaimer, and treat publication or grant year as a proxy for filing year:
  - The 2002-era "Internet refrigerator" (US6393848B2) has almost certainly expired
  - Samsung's 2008 mobile-terminal inventory grant (US7448546B2) has probably expired or is about to
  - US 9.6M–9.9M grants (2017–2018) and the 2016 publications were probably filed 2014–2016, so they run to about 2034–2036 if maintenance fees were paid
  - Amazon's spoilage family was published in 2017, so it was probably filed about 2016 and runs to about 2036
  - LG's before/after comparison application was filed in 2019, so about 2039 if granted
- **What this means for freedom to operate (high level; lawyer needed):**
  1. Many claims recite "a refrigerator comprising…". A retrofit maker does not sell a refrigerator, which may help against direct infringement. But the user assembles camera and fridge, which raises induced or contributory infringement questions. Claims written to a *camera device* or *door-pocket holder* (the EP2975344A1 / US20160057394A1 family) could read directly on a pocket- or shelf-mounted retrofit unit.
  2. Method claims ("capture after door closes, compare before and after images, identify food, notify a terminal") can read on the cloud and app side whatever the hardware. LG's US20200003482A1 describes the founder's planned diff approach and is the first publication to check for grant status and claim scope.
  3. Estimating freshness from **time since first seen plus a shelf-life table** is technically different from Amazon's **imaging-based spoilage** detection. That is a plausible lower-risk design choice, unconfirmed until someone reads Amazon's granted claims.
  4. Samsung now markets alerts based on storage duration. It may hold dwell-time filings that this search did not surface.
  5. Patents are national. US patents matter for selling in the US; Canadian sales are affected only by Canadian patents, which were not searched.
- **A start-up's own patent position is weak.** Prior art since 2014 is dense, which limits what a newcomer could claim on camera placement or triggering. Any defensible claim would sit in narrow specifics, such as the multi-camera fusion method or the uncertainty-and-ask workflow, and even those are unproven.

### Gaps
- Assignees, priority dates, grant status and legal status (lapsed or in force) for most of the listed numbers; Google Patents and USPTO could not be opened directly.
- No patents assigned to Smarter Applications or Liebherr turned up in two targeted searches, and none explicitly claiming in-fridge dwell time as a freshness input. Absence in search summaries is weak evidence.
- Samsung's own AI Vision Inside filings, including dwell time, items going in and out, and "hold the item up to the camera".
- Haier/GE Appliances' FridgeFocus and Scan-to-List filings, and Hisense's 2026 vision filings.
- Canadian (CA) patent counterparts.
- A professional freedom-to-operate search, priced and scoped by a patent agent or lawyer (registered in Canada and the US).

## 6. Synthesis: where incumbents are weak, what a start-up could defend, and what it can't

### Takeaway
"AI recognises what you put in the fridge and warns when it's getting old" is now table stakes for premium fridges (Samsung with Gemini since May 2026) and free on phones (Gemini Live, Amazon's photo-to-cart demo). The idea itself is not defensible, and retrofit cameras have failed three or four times on price, recognition, phone substitution and cloud shutdowns. What is open: existing fridges of any brand (where every appliance maker is absent since Liebherr quit), whole-house coverage (door bins, freezer, pantry, second fridge), Canada, and trust (local-first data that survives a shutdown). Each of these is a positioning wedge, not a moat.

### Cited Findings
- The market leader now offers the core feature: Gemini recognition of 2,000+ foods, warnings "based on how long they have been stored", and Instacart reordering. It is US-first on select US$3,399+ fridges — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2167892/samsungs-bespoke-update-is-big-step-towards-a-useful-ai-for-your-fridge/); [Samsung Global Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-expands-ai-capabilities-of-bespoke-ai-refrigerator-family-hub-with-major-updates); [Techlicious](https://www.techlicious.com/blog/samsung-bespoke-fridge-vision-ai-with-google-gemini/)

### Inferences
**Who else is doing this, or could:**

| Player | Threat level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Samsung | High | Same feature set, already shipping; could extend to other brands via SmartThings (no evidence it plans to) |
| Amazon / Alexa+ | High | Photo plus purchase history plus cart; owns checkout |
| Google / Gemini | High | Free; Samsung's engine; Gemini Live on every Android phone |
| AI "scan your fridge" phone apps | Medium–high | Zero hardware cost, many entrants |
| LG, Hisense, GE | Medium | Built-in, premium-only, brand-locked |
| Whirlpool, Electrolux, Bosch, Miele | Low as competitors | Potential licensees instead |
| Smarter | Low | Still has the hardware and Chefling, but evidently fading |
| Home Assistant DIY community | Low commercially | Sets a free baseline for technical users |

**What killed previous attempts:**
- Recognition failed before 2024; that is now solved for everyone.
- A single fixed camera with blind spots.
- Value a phone photo replicates.
- US$69–149 hardware plus subscription resistance.
- Dependence on a vendor cloud that shut down.
- Raises too small to fund hardware, cloud and support.

**Angles a start-up could plausibly defend (inference, each unproven):**
1. **Existing fridges of any brand.** Every appliance maker is absent from retrofit since Liebherr quit, and fridges in homes far outnumber new premium ones (the installed-base ratio was not researched). Defensibility comes only from being the trusted, supported product, not from technology.
2. **Whole-house coverage.** The multi-camera design for door bins and drawers, plus phone scans of the freezer and pantry, targets exactly what built-in cameras miss. It is hard for a single appliance maker to do across brands. But door-pocket camera devices are patented (EP2975344A1 family), so a freedom-to-operate check is needed.
3. **An uncertainty-aware correction loop.** "Guess, then ask when unsure", with human edits that stick, builds a per-household labelled history of dwell times against actual discards. It could become a data asset over time; on day one it is easy to copy.
4. **Canada-first.** Samsung–Instacart is US-only, and Gemini fridge features reach other countries "in phases". Health Canada shelf-life data and Canadian grocer integrations (via Instacart's developer platform, open to Canadian residents) are a timing wedge, not a moat.
5. **Trust and local-first operation.** No subscription for basic recognition, data export, and keeps working if the cloud dies: the direct answer to the HNGRYnsite and Yummly shutdowns. It is a marketing differentiator that others can copy.
6. **Licensing or white-label** to laggards (Whirlpool, Electrolux, Bosch, Miele) or to grocers, as a business-to-business exit. This is plausibly more realistic than selling hardware to consumers.

**Angles a start-up cannot defend:**
- Food recognition accuracy, which Gemini and GPT-class models have commoditised.
- Owning the shopping list or cart, which Amazon, Instacart and Samsung are fighting over.
- Basic camera hardware or triggering, which is densely patented since 2014, cheap to clone, and has failed repeatedly.
- "Detects spoilage" claims, which no shipping product substantiates and which carry imaging-based spoilage patent risk (Amazon family) plus liability.

**Fit with the founder (inference):** better-funded teams failed at the hardware-plus-cloud-plus-support stack (Smarter raised about US$10M). For a solo, part-time founder the lower-risk sequence is phone-first software with an optional camera, matching the background report's plan. Hardware should be treated as a later bet, after evidence that households keep the list current for weeks.

### Gaps
- Willingness-to-pay data for a retrofit camera in 2026, and the size of the installed base of fridges in Canada and the US. Neither was researched; another researcher may cover the market.
- Regulatory and certification costs for a retrofit device (ISED/FCC radio certification, cold-environment batteries, food-contact materials). Not researched.
- Any evidence that households stick with automatic tracking for more than a few weeks. Earlier research found manual apps had no measurable effect on household waste, which is background, not re-verified.
- Business-to-business niches (office or shared fridges, student residences, food banks, care homes): plausible wedges, but no sources gathered in this pass.
